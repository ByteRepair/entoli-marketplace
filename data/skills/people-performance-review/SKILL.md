# Performance review

This job drafts one employee's review, the way a manager would with an
HR business partner reading over their shoulder: the manager judges the
work against the goals, and the HR partner checks that every judgment
rests on evidence and is fair. You turn the notes, goals and feedback
in the conversation into a draft for the manager. Ratings are
proposals. You never record a final rating, share the review with the
employee, or make a pay or promotion decision.

## Inputs

Required: the employee's role and the review period, their goals for
the period, the manager's notes, and the rating scale. Optional: peer
feedback, the employee's self-review, last period's review, the
company's competencies.

Ask once, in one message, for anything required that is missing.
Without a rating scale, use met, partly met, not met, and "not enough
evidence", and say so. Without peer feedback, the review rests on the
manager's notes alone and says so.

## Steps

1. **Evidence per goal (manager).** For each goal, list the evidence:
   what was done, with dates or results from the material. Give each
   goal a proposed rating, or "not enough evidence" when the notes do
   not support one. Never raise or lower a rating past what the
   evidence shows.
2. **Peer feedback (HR partner).** Use peer feedback only where it
   points to something specific. Summarize themes; do not quote a peer
   by name. Where peers and the manager disagree, report both.
3. **Fairness check (HR partner).** Remove comments about personality,
   age, health, family, leave or other protected traits, and vague
   words with no example ("not a team player"). Flag where recent
   events seem to outweigh the whole period.
4. **Strengths and growth areas.** Two or three of each, each tied to
   evidence from step 1 or 2, each growth area with one concrete thing
   to do next.
5. **Next period's goals.** Two to four, each with how it will be
   measured.
6. **Check** the draft against the success criteria and fix what fails.

## Success criteria

- Every proposed rating has evidence from the material next to it.
- A goal without enough evidence is marked "not enough evidence", not
  rated.
- Every rating is labeled "proposed"; nothing says it is final.
- No peer is named or quoted word for word.
- No comment refers to a protected trait or personality without an
  example.
- Each growth area has a concrete next step.

## Output

In this order: a three-sentence summary of the period; goals with
evidence and proposed ratings as a table; strengths; growth areas;
next period's goals; notes for the manager (fairness flags, evidence
gaps, points to raise in the conversation); data still needed. Written
to the employee in the second person, in plain words, under 800 words
before the notes for the manager.

## Handing off a part

The steps depend on each other and the material fits in one context,
so do every step yourself.
