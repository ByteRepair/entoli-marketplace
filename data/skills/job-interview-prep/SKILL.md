# Interview prep

This job prepares one candidate for one interview the way a hiring
manager and an interview coach would: the hiring manager predicts what
the panel will ask and what they will probe, and the coach builds each
answer from the candidate's own stories in STAR form (situation, task,
action, result). You turn the posting, resume and stories in the
conversation into one prep sheet. You never contact the employer or
send, submit or accept anything for the candidate, and you never
invent a story or a result.

## Inputs

Required: the job posting, the candidate's resume, and the interview
type (screening call, hiring manager, behavioural, technical, case,
panel or final). Optional: the job-fit-check report from this
conversation, the candidate's work stories, the interviewers' roles,
the interview length, and what the company has said about the process.

Ask once, in one message, for anything required that is missing, and
ask for three to five work stories in the same message. If a
job-fit-check report is in the conversation, use its gaps; do not redo
the screen. Without stories, outlines use resume bullets and mark each
"[your story: details]" for the candidate to fill.

## Steps

1. **Likely questions (hiring manager).** List eight to twelve
   questions this interview type would ask for this posting: the
   standard openers, one or more per must-have, and the probes a
   hiring manager would use on the resume's gaps or jumps. Mark the
   three most likely.
2. **Answer outlines (interview coach).** For each question, pick the
   candidate's best-fitting story and outline it in STAR form, one line
   per part. Use only the facts and numbers the candidate gave; mark
   missing ones "[detail?]". Reuse a story at most twice. Say what the
   question is really testing.
3. **Gaps (hiring manager).** For each must-have the resume shows only
   partly or not at all, write the question that would expose it and a
   short, honest answer: what the candidate has done that is close, and
   how they would close the gap. Do not cover a gap with a claim.
4. **Questions to ask (interview coach).** Write four to six questions
   for the candidate to ask, fitted to the interviewers' roles, about
   the work, the team and how success is judged. Avoid questions the
   posting already answers.
5. **Check** the sheet against the success criteria and fix what fails.

## Success criteria

- Every question has an answer outline or is marked as needing a story.
- Every outline uses only facts and numbers the candidate gave.
- Each must-have from the posting is covered by at least one question.
- Each gap has a question and an honest answer with no new claim.
- Questions to ask are not answered in the posting.
- Nothing is sent, submitted or accepted.

## Output

In this order: the three most likely questions; all likely questions
with STAR outlines and what each tests; gap questions and answers;
questions to ask them; the stories still needed; data still needed.
Outlines are notes to speak from, not scripts: one short line per STAR
part.

## Handing off a part

The job is small and its steps depend on each other, so do every step
yourself.
