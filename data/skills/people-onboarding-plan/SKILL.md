# Onboarding plan

This job plans one new hire's first 90 days, the way a small team
would: the hiring manager sets what good looks like at 30, 60 and 90
days, the people operations partner covers paperwork, people and
culture, and the IT administrator lists the access and equipment. You
turn the role, team and tools in the conversation into one plan. You
never send invites, create accounts, grant access, or contact the new
hire.

## Inputs

Required: the role and level, the start date, the manager, the team,
and the tools and systems the person will use. Optional: a buddy, key
people to meet, the first project, company onboarding sessions, the
location or remote policy, working hours.

Ask once, in one message, for anything required that is missing.
Without a named buddy, the plan has a "buddy: to assign" task for the
manager before day one. Without a first project, the 30-day goals say
what the manager must choose by the end of week two.

## Steps

1. **Before day one (IT and people operations).** List every account,
   tool and piece of equipment from the inputs with its owner and a
   ready-by date before the start date. Add the paperwork and the
   welcome message, each with an owner.
2. **First week, day by day (people operations).** For each of the
   first five working days: what happens, who runs it, and what the
   new hire should have done by the end of the day. Keep day one light:
   setup, the manager, the buddy, the team.
3. **30, 60 and 90 days (hiring manager).** For each stage, two to four
   goals the manager can check: learn (30), contribute (60), own (90).
   Tie them to the role and the first project; do not invent
   performance targets the inputs do not support.
4. **Checkpoints.** A short weekly meeting with the manager in month
   one, and a review at days 30, 60 and 90, each with the questions to
   ask both ways.
5. **Check** the plan against the success criteria and fix what fails.

## Success criteria

- Every task has an owner (a role or a named person from the inputs,
  never a handle) and a date.
- Every tool in the inputs has an access task due before the start
  date.
- Each of the first five working days is planned, counted from the
  stated start date and skipping weekends.
- Each of the 30, 60 and 90-day stages has two to four goals someone
  can check.
- There are checkpoints at days 30, 60 and 90.

## Output

In this order: a two-line summary (role, start date, manager, buddy);
before day one as a checklist; the first week as a table by day; the
30, 60 and 90-day goals; the checkpoints; data still needed. Dates are
real dates, not "day 12". Plain words, written so the new hire could
read it too.

## Handing off a part

This job is usually small enough for one pass; do every step yourself.
When many new hires start together, one plan per hire may run as a
parallel sub-agent, each briefed with that hire's inputs.
