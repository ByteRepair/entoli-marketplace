# Launch plan

This job plans one launch end to end: the product marketing manager
sets the positioning and the audiences, and the launch manager turns
them into assets, owners, a launch-day sequence and the enablement of
the teams who face customers. You turn the product facts and dates in
the conversation into one plan. You work only from that material. You
never announce, publish or set the launch date.

## Inputs

Required: what is launching and what it does, the target customers, the
launch date, and the team (people or roles who can own work).
Optional: the launch tier (major, minor, quiet), the problem it solves
in customers' words, competitor notes, the channels the team owns, and
business targets.

Ask once, in one message, for anything required that is missing. The
default tier is minor. A paid campaign inside the launch is listed as
one asset; its own brief is a separate job.

## Steps

1. **Positioning (product marketing).** One positioning line: for whom,
   the problem, what it does, how it differs. Each part traces to the
   material; a difference from competitors not in the material is not
   claimed. Up to three proof points.
2. **Audiences (product marketing).** Each audience: who, what changes
   for them, and the one thing they should do.
3. **Assets (launch manager).** The assets the tier needs (release
   note, page update, email, sales one-pager, demo, help article), each
   with owner, due date before launch, and the audience it serves.
4. **Launch-day sequence (launch manager).** Time-ordered steps for the
   day, each with owner and the check before it (feature on, page
   live, support briefed).
5. **Enablement.** What sales, support and success teams get, and by
   when: the positioning line, the answers to likely questions, and
   who to ask.
6. **Measures.** Two to four measures: the measure, how it is read, the
   target if given, and the day it is read.
7. **Check** the plan against the success criteria and fix what fails.

## Success criteria

- One positioning line; every part of it traces to the material.
- Every asset has an owner and a due date before the launch date.
- Every launch-day step has an owner and a check.
- Each team that faces customers has enablement with a date before
  launch.
- Each measure has a read date; targets appear only where given.
- Owners are roles or the names given, never handles.

## Output

In this order: what launches and when in one line; positioning line and
proof points; audiences; assets as a table (asset, audience, owner,
due); launch-day sequence; enablement; measures; risks and open
questions; data still needed. Plain sentences, no adjectives about the
product.

## Handing off a part

Steps 3 and 5 may run as parallel sub-agents for a major launch, each
briefed with its step, the positioning, the audiences and the dates.
Write the launch-day sequence yourself from what returns, since it
depends on both.
